Steps for your recipe:
1. dummy code any categorical predictors
2. create interactions between
• type and shucked_weight,
• longest_shell and diameter,
• shucked_weight and shell_weight
3. center all predictors, and
4. scale all predictors.

On testing data the Linear model outperformed, having a higher R2 and lower RMSE and MAE. I would
assume this is due to KNN relying on being able to see the answers to the training set, not allowing it to
generalize on new data. KNN in testing falls behind, where the relationship found in the linear model during
training holds up well to new data. I was initially surprised that KNN outperformed in training but Linear
outperformed on testing, since I expected the better training model to also do better on testing. Looking at
the numbers, though, this is indicative of overfitting. When KNN predicts the training set, each point counts
itself as one of its 7 neighbors, so training R2 is inflated and falls to 0.483 on new data. The less flexible
linear model scores about the same on both, 0.552 training vs. 0.585 testing, so training performance alone
doesn’t show which model will generalize better.
